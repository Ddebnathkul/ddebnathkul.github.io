About me

From stellar atmospheres to outflows.

I am a PhD researcher in the EQUATION group at KU Leuven, supervised by Prof. Jon O. Sundqvist and Dr. Nicolas Moens. My research combines radiation hydrodynamics, radiative transfer and high-performance computing to understand massive-star atmospheres and winds in 3D.

I use unified multidimensional atmosphere and wind simulations to continuously model the deep, optically thick envelope layers into the supersonic outflow. I am interested in how opacity-driven turbulence, radiation-matter interactions and line driving shape the atmospheres of massive stars and their impact of the outcome spectra, and global properties such as mass-loss rates.

Methods & software

Models, tools, and data.

Radiation-hydrodynamics

MPI-AMRVAC

MPI-AMRVAC (Message Passing Interface - Adaptive Mesh Refinement - Versatile Advection Code) is an open-source, parallel computational framework for multidimensional (magneto)hydrodynamics. Its adaptive mesh refinement allows high spatial resolution in regions with strong gradients and shocks.

I use its radiation-hydrodynamics module with currently a flux-limited diffusion (FLD) approach to model the coupling between radiation and matter in hot, massive stars. Our 2D and 3D simulations, simultaneously model the deep, optically thick envelope, through the iron-opacity-bump and photosphere, into the supersonic, line-driven wind. These unified models allow us to study atmospheric structure formation, velocity fluctuations, and wind dynamics self-consistently.

Radiative transfer · Work in progress

STARFRUIT

I am building in collaboration a frequency-dependent radiative transfer method for multidimensional massive star atmospheres and winds, incorporating the full continuum opacity (including bound-free, free-free and electron scattering contributions) together with line blocking/blanketting, and taking line-scattering into account.

Ongoing development
Opacity calculations

MFORCE

MFORCE calculates line opacity multipliers and mean opacities for hot, massive star atmospheres and winds from a database of millions of spectral lines. The original method computes flux-weighted line opacities and line-force parameters in accelerating media. Recent extensions incorporate approximate NLTE effects and line scattering to calculate separate flux-, energy- and Planck-weighted opacities for radiative momentum and thermal coupling for radiation-hydrodynamical simulations.

Poniatowski et al. (2022) ↗Debnath et al. (2026) ↗
Mass-loss estimator

LIME

Line-driven wind Iterative Mass-loss Estimator - a fast, physics-based tool for predicting the mass-loss rates of hot, massive stars with arbitrary chemical abundances.
